The hard part is not the reading.

Someone writes to an organisation and asks for everything it holds about them. A dismissed employee, a parent, a customer in a dispute. The organisation has one month to find it across email, shared drives and chat, and to hand it over with everything about other people taken out.

That is days of reading, one document at a time. But the reading is not what goes wrong. What goes wrong is the redaction that lets another person's details through, and the moment the requester says something is missing and there is nothing to show that it is not.

What you get back

The response

The documents found for the request, with other people's information redacted and the redactions burned in, ready for you to send.

The search record

What was searched and how, what was left out and why, and the decisions made along the way. One document you can put on the file.

The missed-document check

A sample drawn from the documents the search did not return, read by a person, with the sample size stated. It is an estimate, not a guarantee. A small clean sample proves little, which is why the size is on the page.

A search you can trust defend.

Since the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, the law says in terms that the search behind a subject access request has to be reasonable and proportionate. That is Article 15(1A) of the UK GDPR. It does not say what such a search looks like. If the search is challenged, it is the organisation that has to justify where it stopped.

On 11 November 2026 the ICO published guidance on requests written with AI. Those requests are longer and broader, and they are still valid. The guidance says you should record your decision about the scope of the search: whether you asked the requester, what you included, what you left out, and why.

From 19 June 2026 every controller must also run a complaints procedure and acknowledge a complaint within 30 days. People keep the right to go to the ICO directly.

What graphlion writes down as the work happens

  • Every search, and how each working set was built
  • Every coding decision, and every time a person overrode a suggestion
  • The exact instruction given to the model, and its version
  • Confidence in bands, always beside the documents behind it
  • The missed-document check, with its sample size

It comes out as one export, not a fortnight of reconstruction.

When they say you missed something, you will have an answer.

The ICO tells a requester who thinks something is missing to list what they believe you hold and to complain to you first. The search record is what you answer with: what was searched, what was left out and why, and what the check of the remainder found.
